The cache fix halves retained live allocations in the three-scan million-file workload and moves expensive cache destruction off the main actor. The retention audit baseline is committed as `6df5a0f`. **Behavior and ownership**

`FileTreeStore.backingStorageID` follows the buffers shared by a parent and its logical scopes. Materialized or transformed trees receive their own identity. The cache uses the existing full `backingNodeCapacity` for its cost and keeps one entry per backing tree. A cached parent serves contained scopes; a wider parent replaces a cached child. This avoids accumulating scope membership bitsets and avoids another reference-count/accounting dictionary.

The 250,000-node budget now evicts older independent trees even when only two scans remain. One newest oversized tree is retained for navigation. This remains a node-based retention policy, not a byte or process-RSS ceiling. A scope with 10,001 visible nodes still retains and is charged for its 1,000,101-node backing tree.

Evicted cache ownership enters a release batch. A main-actor task starts the background handoff after synchronous mutation frames unwind, preventing their temporary copies from becoming the final owner. One serial utility worker takes the batch under a short lock and destroys it outside that lock. Cache lookups and invalidation stay synchronous on the main actor.

New admission pauses while cleanup is pending. Repeated clear/store calls can retire only the existing cache contents and the entry admitted by the mutation that began cleanup; they cannot accumulate successive new scans in a task queue. Incoming scans can still be displayed, but may miss cache admission and invalidate stale cached entries. Folder restoration asynchronously waits for cleanup and retains its containing parent before publishing a scope. Selecting an already displayed target also reuses it without requiring a cache hit. Returning to an evicted independent scan can require rescanning.

**Paired measurements**

Three rounds ran on the same Apple M5 MacBook Air, 10 cores, 32 GiB RAM, macOS 26.6.2, Xcode 26.6, and local APFS. A generated fixture contained 100 directories with 10,000 empty files each. Cache, scope, and navigation scenarios each ran before and after in fresh optimized Release processes, with ordering reversed in the second round: 18 processes and 30 completed scans. Builds and fixture creation/cleanup did not overlap measurement. Filesystem caches and machine load were uncontrolled.

The before bundle preserves the audit benchmark and original cache. The after benchmark awaits background cleanup before reporting cache-only ownership and before its final 100 ms settling delay. Those waits are outside the synchronous operation timers; baseline destruction already completed synchronously before the equivalent checkpoints. These are direct cache/navigation model probes, not complete SwiftUI frame traces.

| Three-scan workload, median | Before | After |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Live malloc after third scan and eviction settle | 1,827.1 MB | 914.3 MB |
| Live malloc with third result built, before insertion | 2,739.9 MB | 1,827.1 MB |
| Process peak RSS | 3,164.3 MB | 2,249.5 MB |
| Live malloc after clearing and settling | 1.44 MB | 1.44 MB |
| RSS after clearing and settling | 2,604.0 MB | 1,930.7 MB |
| Physical footprint after clearing and settling | 1,471.1 MB | 797.6 MB |

Live cached allocations fell 50%; the third-result ownership checkpoint fell 33%; process peak RSS fell 29%. A single cached scope still retains approximately 914.5 MB, as expected from its shared backing tree. Across all 18 processes, settled live malloc returned to 1.444–1.448 MB. Residual RSS and footprint remain allocator memory; every benchmark-only pressure-relief call returned zero. No allocator-purge call was added to the app.

| Synchronous main-actor operation, median | Before | After |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Third cache insertion, evicting one million-node tree | 462.045 ms | 0.044 ms |
| Clear remaining cache (two trees before, one after) | 804.754 ms | 0.023 ms |
| Clear cache owning only a small scope of a million-node tree | 416.398 ms | 0.050 ms |
| Clear navigation immediately after clearing its parent cache | 427.199 ms | 0.244 ms |

Before third-insertion times ranged from 361.9–478.7 ms; after times ranged from 0.032–0.052 ms. These timings measure whole operations and vary from the earlier audit's baseline. They show that expensive release moved out of the main-actor call; they do not show that destruction itself became free or establish a scanner-throughput improvement.

The navigation result relies on clearing the cache and navigation in the same synchronous turn: the queued cache owner survives until navigation drops its copy. Owners outside this cache can still perform the final release later, including on the main actor. This change does not govern every chart, browser, or app-shutdown lifetime.
